Why the Feast matters

A meal can do more than fill a plate.

Big Street Feast is built around a simple belief: food should bring people together, and nobody should be left out of the moment.

Created around World Food Day, the initiative takes that belief into the streets. It brings cooks, volunteers, food businesses, organisations and communities together to share free meals while drawing attention to food access and the everyday realities behind it.

For Chef Abbys, the Feast is not only about how many plates leave the serving line. It is also about what can happen when people use what they have, the platforms they have built and the resources they can reach to make a difference.

The Feast, edition by edition

It started in Accra.
Then the table grew.

Each edition has brought a bigger circle of people into the work. What began in one city expanded into a two-city effort for World Food Day 2026.

THE FIRST EDITION2025

Accra came to the table.

The first Big Street Feast took the idea to the streets of Accra. In a MyJoyOnline piece published ahead of the second edition, Chef Abbys reflected that the first outing had served more than 15,000 people.

Accra, Ghana
THE SECOND EDITION2026

The table reached two cities.

On Friday, 16 October 2026, the Feast returned for World Food Day with an expanded effort across Accra and Kumasi. Post-event coverage reported that more than 50,000 people were reached.

Accra & Kumasi, Ghana
Out where people are

A Feast that moves with the city.

The 2026 edition was organised to reach people across Accra and Kumasi, using fixed distribution points and mobile teams to extend the reach beyond one venue.

ACCRA

Ghud Park at Accra Mall served as the main distribution point. Other reported locations included Madina, Kwabenya, Kasoa and Fadama, with mobile trucks extending into additional areas.

KUMASI

The second edition brought the initiative to Kumasi as well, making the Feast a two-city World Food Day effort.

Distribution details reported by MX24 on 19 October 2026.

What the Feast stands for

More than food.
A feeling that stays.

The Feast is one practical expression of a bigger conversation about how people, businesses and communities can contribute to a more caring food culture.

Food access

Making free meals available in public spaces brings the conversation about affordability and access into everyday life.

Shared dignity

A meal should be offered with warmth and respect. The person receiving it is part of the gathering, not a statistic.

Collective action

When volunteers, food businesses, brands and community organisations each play a part, the impact can travel further.
